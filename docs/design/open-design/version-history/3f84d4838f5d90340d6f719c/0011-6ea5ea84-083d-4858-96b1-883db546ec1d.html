        Executive summary

        The constraints best supported by this pass's evidence are grid connection, documented almost entirely through U.S. data, and the concentration of battery supply chains in China. In the United States, 2,600 GW of proposed generation and storage projects were waiting for grid access at the end of 2023, typical waits stretch to five years or more, fewer than a quarter of queued projects are expected to reach completion at current rates, and delay carries quantified costs to consumers. [8][10][13]

        On the supply side, the IEA reports that China processed 70 to 95 percent of global lithium, cobalt, phosphate and graphite in 2024, is projected to remain dominant through 2035, and estimates that full implementation of China's export controls could put $6.5 trillion in annual production outside China at risk. [1][2][4]

        FERC's Order 2023 and Order 2023-A introduced queue reform, including stronger financial and site-control requirements and improved storage modeling, and U.S. queue capacity fell for the first time in 2024, but the evidence does not show the bottleneck resolved. [13]

        Several constraint families raised in review remain unevidenced in this pass: tariffs and trade barriers, siting, permitting and fire-code compliance, wholesale market eligibility and accreditation rules, non-U.S. connection regimes, equipment lead times, and financing conditions.

        Grid connection as the binding timing constraint

        Grid connection is the most fully evidenced constraint on grid-scale battery deployment in this pass, although the grid-side evidence is confined to the United States. As of the end of 2023, 2,600 GW of proposed U.S. generation and storage projects were waiting for grid access, with typical wait times stretching to five years or more. Research cited from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory puts the average wait in U.S. interconnection queues at five years and indicates that, at current rates, less than a quarter of the energy projects entering those queues will make it to completion. [13]

        Aggregated reporting across U.S. ISOs and RTOs places over 2,000 GW in queues, with delays stretching from three to seven years in some markets. The delay is not limited to generators, as Google has reported potential transmission grid connection delays of up to 12 years for new data centers. [11]

        Critical minerals and supply-chain concentration

        Battery deployment also depends on a supply chain whose midstream is heavily concentrated in one country. The IEA reports that China retained its dominance over the midstream and downstream EV and storage battery supply chain in 2024, processing 70 to 95 percent of global lithium, cobalt, phosphate and graphite. [2]

        The trend in the evidence runs toward more exposure, not less: excluding rare earths, the average share of mineral supply held by China rose to 72 percent in 2025 from 70 percent in 2023, and the number of mineral tariff categories covered by China's export controls has tripled since 2023. [4][6]

        • The IEA reports that China retained its dominance over the midstream and downstream EV and storage battery supply chain in 2024, processing 70â€“95% of global lithium, cobalt, phosphate and graphite. [2] confidence 0.88
        • FERC's Order 2023 mandated U.S. interconnection queue reform and included stronger financial and site control requirements to discourage speculative projects, plus improved storage modeling, with Order 2023-A reaffirming most of it. [13] confidence 0.92
        • For every $1 billion in transmission investments that is delayed, consumers lose between $150 million and $370 million in net benefits per year of delay. [11] confidence 0.90

        Uncertainty and conflicting evidence

        Several things should temper confidence in the picture above. The grid-side evidence is U.S.-only: non-U.S. connection regimes were sought in this pass but produced no verified claim or retrieved finding. One claim was contradicted: an assertion that the U.S. queue had swelled to a 2,600 GW backlog as of 2026 with median waits approaching five years was rejected, while a verified claim places 2,600 GW in the queue as of the end of 2023. Queue magnitude estimates also conflict, and several central numbers rest on sources this pass flagged low confidence.
