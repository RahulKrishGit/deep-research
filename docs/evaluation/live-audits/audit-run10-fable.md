### 2. Rules written for P6 that over-reached here

1. **The dissent cue list.** "however" (and the re-ask's "qualifies") were added to catch "Later field surveys found little evidence …" — revisionist scholarship against a settled step. In regulatory and guidance text "however" introduces a proviso on the same body's own statement. The mechanism turned agreement into a dispute and put it in the bottom line (D2).
2. **The credit guard's notion of a credit.** Built so that no point "names no one" (run 7's S043), it accepts pages, hosts and reporting verbs. The constraints answer form asks for "the instrument, rule, or authority that imposes it" — "Article 53(1)(a) requires" is the correct credit for a rule and the guard refuses it; "The Commission states" is the correct credit for guidance and a regex written for "the United States" deletes it (D3, D5, D7).
3. **The housekeeping rule.** Written to stop copyright lines and revision stamps printing as points, it also swallows a reproduced provision's own "this text has been amended" notice — on a rules-with-dates question, the one disclaimer that is an answer (D4).
4. **The planner's why/how rule is shape-specific.** It fixed P6's over-enumeration for mechanism questions; on a rules question the planner over-enumerated in the same way and no rule stopped it (D1). The fix is not a rules-specific rule but the general form of the existing one: on any question, the thing the question asks for is the required target in the question's words; whatever the plan names to find it (episodes, provisions, bodies, examples) is a lead or an optional target.
5. **The self-declared-kind detector** did not over-reach; it under-reached: four P6-shaped categories, none for the self-declarations a document about rules actually makes (reproduces an instrument; not binding / not an official position; amended or consolidated to a date; tool-generated). A general detector returns the sentence and a kind for any "this document is …" statement, and lets the evaluator score it.
